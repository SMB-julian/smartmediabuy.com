How a deal gets built

Four steps, roughly two weeks.

This is a real sequence — each step depends on the one before it. Most of the elapsed time is deal sync and trafficking on your side, not build time on ours.

01

Define the outcome

We start from what the campaign is judged on — not the targeting spec. Reach at a CPM ceiling, completion rate, qualified traffic, in-store visits: the metric decides which of the three inputs above we lean on.

02

Assemble the package

Inventory selected across our exchange seats, segments applied, rules written in. We forecast available impressions and a clearing range before anything is trafficked, so the deal isn't scoped on hope.

03

Sync and traffic

The deal ID syncs to your DSP seat. Your team traffics it like any other PMP. No new contract with each publisher, no tags, no engineering work on your side.

04

Read it and revise

Win rate, bid density, clearing price and delivery by domain, reported back at the level the deal was built on — so when something underdelivers you can see whether it was the floor, the audience or the supply.

What lands in your seat

One deal ID, fully specified.

  • Works in the DSP you already use. Deal IDs sync to the major buying platforms. Nothing about your workflow changes.
  • Domain-level delivery reporting. You see where it ran, what it cleared at, and what didn't bid — not a rolled-up summary.

On fees

Ours is a line item, not a spread.

Curation has an unflattering reputation in some quarters, and it's earned: too many curators add a fee for loading a site list into an exchange. Three commitments, in writing, before any deal goes live.

Stated, not derived

A flat, disclosed fee agreed up front. We don't take a variable margin between what you pay and what the publisher is paid.

Visible in the chain

We're declared as an intermediary in sellers.json and the SupplyChain object. You can audit the path we sit in.

Drop it if it isn't earning

If a curated deal isn't beating the open-auction benchmark you'd otherwise buy, we'd rather you turn it off than keep paying for it.
